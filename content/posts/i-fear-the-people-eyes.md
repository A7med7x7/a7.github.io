Growing up, every friendship I formed lived in oscillation. I would get close, then disappear. I would respond warmly for a while, then withdraw without warning. It became a pattern so consistent that it almost defined my relationships. Eventually, most people left, not because they hated me, but because disappearing repeatedly makes staying exhausting. you cannot hold on to someone who keeps stepping back into the shadows.

There was only one exception, he was like a boomerang. That is the most accurate description I can give. no matter how distant I became, no matter how deliberately I pushed him away, he returned. I did not want people to know me, and I did not have the emotional capacity to know them either. even one friend felt like too much. I barely had space for him in my life. I enjoyed my time alone at home more than anything else. When he visited and I felt overwhelmed, I would ask my mother to tell him I was asleep. I hid from him inside my own house.

He used to get angry at me for disappearing, for not telling him what I was going through, for vanishing without explanation. but surprisingly, he never left. he kept coming back.

At fourteen or fifteen, I finally confronted something in my heart. I admitted that he was my friend. he had been there since we were single digits in age. for the first time, although I had acted like a friend before, I never actually [attached the word](https://a7med7x7.github.io/posts/adding-new-friends-system/#:~:text=If%20you%20keep%20saying%20everyone%20is%20your%20friend%2C%20you%20will%20end%20up%20wounded%20alot.) to someone. that moment made him the first real friend I ever consciously named. And he was the best of them.

He still tells me that I am lying when I say I did not count him as a friend earlier. he insists I always did, my actions where saying so. And maybe he is right. I loved him from the beginning. but the oscillation was still there. love did not remove the pattern. It only survived it.

## Something Off About That Friend

Eventually, our group became three. Altijani joined us. we were completely different personalities, yet we shared joy and suffering together. on the surface, it looked stable. but the pattern in me did not disappear. It simply adapted.

Later, I got to know someone else. A friendship started developing after about a year of knowing each other. It was not smooth. In fact, I kept avoiding him in the beginning, even though the friendship was clearly forming. I had only one issue with him, and it sounds ridiculous when I say it out loud: his mouth was sweet.

He used affirmations. He used warm words. he openly described the good he saw in me. that was normal. healthy, even. the problem was not him. the problem was me, it is now that I discovered it.

I kept avoiding him until he learned to respect my walls. only later, after I began to understand myself better, did I realize what bothered me. he brought emotions into conversation. he verbalized appreciation. he named feelings. that was something I was not trained to handle.

With Abd Alwahab and Altijani, our friendships were built on action. we never sat down and told each other how much we loved one another. we never ritualized affection. we never analyzed the friendship. It existed, through shared time and shared experience. and that was precisely why it worked for me. It did not require emotional exposure.

It succeeded because it did not demand what I was afraid of giving.


## Never the Spotlight

I have always had the ability to lead conversations. I can direct them anywhere except toward myself. I can ask questions endlessly. I can analyze topics deeply. I can make people laugh. I can shift attention. but when the light starts turning toward me, I instinctively redirect it.

When our computer science batch at the Faculty of Informatics and Mathematics at the University of Khartoum created a group chat, which I helped initiate, there was excitement about getting to know each other. they decided that each day someone would introduce themselves online and answer questions from the group. I was terrified.

I avoided it strategically. I delayed. I stayed quiet. I hoped the idea would fade. Eventually, everyone got busy and forgot about my turn. they did manage to corner me later at the university and force me to introduce myself in person, but the experience was not as catastrophic as I had imagined. At least I survived.

Presentations were similar. I loved creating PowerPoint slides. I enjoyed structuring ideas. I loved preparing content. but presenting it? standing there? being watched? that was different.

While most people eagerly joined university clubs and debates, I hesitated. My contributions usually happened because someone else pushed me forward. I never joined the debates hosted by the Islamic club at the faculty. Instead, I would sit with [Mohammed Othaman](https://web.facebook.com/profile.php?id=61566402833923), helping him prepare, discussing ideas for his next debate. I preferred being behind the curtain.

I never wanted to join the [faculty students association](https://web.facebook.com/FmsAssociation1), but my friend [Malik](https://web.facebook.com/Mal1k124) insisted. I never wanted to lead [SSE](http://web.facebook.com/uofksse), but I was nominated. I never wanted to welcome the new batch 022 in orientation, but my colleagues convinced me to run a workshop. 

I would show up. Then disappear. one day, my friend Amro called me Batman. I would appear unexpectedly, contribute intensely, then vanish again. the behavior I developed as a child followed me into adulthood without me realizing.

Years later, after being [diagnosed with panic attacks](https://a7med7x7.github.io/posts/days-i-still-dont-understand/#:~:text=told%20me%20it%20was%20a%20panic%20attack) and [severe social anxiety](https://a7med7x7.github.io/posts/days-i-still-dont-understand/#:~:text=And%20suddenly%2C%20I%20had%20social%20anxiety), I understood something that had always been there. I was always afraid of the spotlight. a few days ago, while searching through old notes, I found something I wrote to relieve stress during one of my anxious seasons. It said:

> I fear the people eyes

Reading it now, I understand it differently. I was afraid of being seen. Afraid of being examined. afraid that if people looked closely enough, they would see something I could not control.

I have told people before, half joking but fully serious, that if you want to lose me as a friend, put me in the spotlight. that is the place I fear the most. not because I cannot perform. but because performance invites visibility. visibility invites people, and people threatens the walls.

## About the Walls

I made it hard [for people to stay in my life](https://a7med7x7.github.io/posts/i-keep-failing-the-people-i-care-about/). you cannot expect someone to remain close to you when you disappear unpredictably. when you return without explanation. when you answer emotionally heavy questions with detachment.

I get this question a lot and it is one of the hardest questions people asked me in relationships, it was simple: “What do I mean to you, Ahmed?”

I was fine with you until you asked that question, I remember my answers clearly. sometimes I packaged them gently: I show care through actions, not labels. If I sound distant, ignore the tone, I will always answer when you call. other times, I was brutally honest. I would say I feel nothing. that I am numb.

I never lied about my emotional state. the problem was not dishonesty. the problem was absence. I genuinely did not feel what others expected me to feel. between the ambiguity of my words and the honesty of my numbness, people got hurt deeply.

It took me years to realize the amount of pain [I caused many of them](https://a7med7x7.github.io/posts/learning-the-truth-about-how-i-love/#:~:text=On%20The%20Harm%20I%20Caused%20Others). the [walls](https://a7med7x7.github.io/posts/elaborate_on_adding_new_friends_system/) I built were not neutral. they protected me, yes, but they also injured others. I wanted people to respect my boundaries and remain present, but I did not offer them emotional access.

I was running the moment someone tried to enter my sphere. I wanted loyalty without vulnerability. presence without exposure. closeness without being known.

No wonder my childhood resulted in one friend ,And even with him, I oscillated.
