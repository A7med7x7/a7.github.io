title = 'I Didnt Know it Was Hope'

Looking back and comparing the now, I thought I've lost both hope and faith.
Not the religious kind of faith, but the faith that there exists a way to make things out.  
That there exists a way.

Hope is a word that I've never stopped and thought about what it means.
I don't see myself as someone hoping for things to go good.
And I couldn't remember if I ever did.

Having hope or having faith are words I can't just swallow.
I just can't understand what they should mean to me.

It's like telling me I should just wait.  
It hints that I should have an anchor of desire hooked around the things I hope to get.

And here is where it gets hard.

I don't know what I want.
I don't know what exactly is the thing that will fix it.
I don't pray to get it, I don't know what it is.

I pray to get relief.
And I don't know if relief is the answer either.

So hope requires you to have something visually in your mind.
By name.
To hook it with.

But I don't have a name for the things I want.
Because I don't know them yet.

Hope contradicts the person I've turned into.

The thirteen years old me learned to do things in order to get them.  
My religion is not praying.
My religion is not hoping.
My religion is doing.

Doing what it takes to get something.  
Whatever it is, name it.

I can't hope for things to change or to have them.
I only work to get things.

But hope doesn't work that way.

Hope is when you can't get the things at the moment.
Instead of working, you anchor your desire to them.

And this is not me either.

I'm too afraid to love things deeply.  
To have a desire.  
To have a hope that I will get it.

It's just the man i've turned into, and hope are parallel lines.

But I haven't been like this forever.

Hope used to visit me.  
Not in all forms, but in one.

There was a hope that things would get better.

That was when I was around fifteen to eighteen.  
I had a faithful heart.  
Guarded with hope.

Not for a particular event.  
Not for relationships.  
Not for things I wanted or had a name for.

But for the uncertainty I had been living in.  
For the void inside me.  
For the meaning I couldn't see.  
For the fragments I couldn't interpret.
 
It used to help me sleep at night.

There was this warmth of hope guarding my heart.

I remembered it today while having an internal dialogue with myself.  
Asking why there are inner tools I used to have that made this terrible feeling inside of me no longer exist.

Where did they go.
What were they.

I wasn't so fragile and weak.
I was much more faithful and hopeful.

And then I got to name it.

I was hopeful.  
I was.
